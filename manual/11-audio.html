11. Audio

Sound travels over the same HDMI cable as the picture, with no extra wiring, and an optional second POKEY gives true stereo to software that knows how to use it.

HDMI audio

POKEY's sound is embedded in the HDMI signal as 48 kHz stereo PCM. Any HDMI monitor, TV or AV receiver with audio output plays it directly. There is nothing to wire and nothing to configure: connect the HDMI cable and turn up the volume on your display.

No analogue outputEarlier versions offered an analogue (sigma-delta) audio output on two header pins. That output has been removed: those pins now carry Joystick 1 Left and Right (see Hardware setup). HDMI is the only audio path.

Dual-POKEY stereo

The Atari's POKEY chip is mono. The classic stereo upgrade for real machines adds a second POKEY at address $D210; software that knows about it plays some voices on the second chip. The board emulates this upgrade: when Stereo is ON, POKEY 1 plays on the left channel and POKEY 2 on the right. When it is OFF the machine behaves like a stock, single-POKEY Atari, in mono.

Stereo is OFF by default, so all existing software behaves exactly as it always has.

Turning it on

  1. Open the OSD (F12 or the S2 button) and choose Options.
  2. Set Stereo to ON. The change applies live — no reboot is needed.
  3. Choose Save changes if you want it to stay on after the next power-up. It is stored in atari.ini as stereo= (0 = OFF, 1 = ON; default 0). See Options & atari.ini.
Enable Stereo before loading stereo softwareStereo-aware programs (players, demos, games with dual-POKEY support) look for the second POKEY when they load. If Stereo is still OFF at that moment they will not find it and will stay in mono, even if you switch Stereo on afterwards. Turn Stereo ON first, then load or restart the program.
